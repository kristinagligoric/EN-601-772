EN 601.772: Advanced Topics in AI for Social Science Discovery

Johns Hopkins University - Fall 2026

This advanced course explores state-of-the-art AI methods for social science research, focusing on LLM-based approaches including synthetic participants, AI-augmented surveys, and computational simulations of human behavior. Students will critically evaluate recent work through paper discussions and an original research project. Projects will involve empirical work with publication potential, such as replication studies comparing synthetic to human data, meta-analyses of field practices, or development of validation frameworks for the emerging field of AI-augmented social science.

Prerequisites: Having taken a graduate-level computer science course in Artificial Intelligence or Machine Learning (including CSS, NLP, Computer Vision, etc.) is expected. Students must be comfortable with reading recent research papers and discussing key concepts and ideas.

Course Goals: Specific outcomes for this course are that students will be able to:

  • Determine appropriate use cases for AI-augmented methods by comparing their strengths and weaknesses against traditional social science approaches (surveys, experiments, qualitative interviews) for specific research questions.
  • Design and execute rigorous studies that assess whether AI-generated data (synthetic survey responses, simulated behaviors, agent interactions) accurately represent human populations and social phenomena.
  • Critically evaluate methodological claims in published research, identifying validity threats, confounds, and limitations in studies using LLMs for social science applications.
  • Implement reproducible research practices including pre-registration, comprehensive reporting of prompts and model parameters, sensitivity analyses, and proper documentation of AI system configurations.
  • Conduct original empirical research that contributes to methodological knowledge in AI-augmented social science, whether through replication studies, validation frameworks, meta-analyses, or novel applications.
